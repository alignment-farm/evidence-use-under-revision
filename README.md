# Evidence use under revision

An independent Construct-2 ancillary study, commissioned 16 September 2026.

**Status: bounded local study completed, 16 September 2026.**

A prespecified 128-update varied-history checkpoint improved complete outcomes
through two authoritative corrections: 65/96 fresh use events, versus 52/96 for
the base and 61/96 for the stronger contextual lesson. Its measured training plus
inference time was lower over this observed sequence, but construction costs,
serving optimizations and unchanged-case regressions limit the repayment claim.
Further training reduced the same trajectory to 45/96. A supplied executable
policy completed 96/96. The broader question and root acceptance remain separate.

## Question

When experience improves an agent's use of explicit evidence, does the acquired
behavior remain useful across successive authoritative corrections, and does
it repay acquisition and maintenance cost against retained contextual lessons?

Construct-2 asks how agents accumulate useful experience across sessions and
where it should live: explicit memory, contextual lessons, executable tools,
model weights or runtime rules. A combination is already the working hypothesis.
This study investigates the useful lifetime of one such allocation: acquired
ways of using evidence alongside explicit, revisable facts and instructions.
It is independent of completed maintenance-selector, memory-placement and
state/support phases; none is restarted by this commission.

## Why this comparison

Local explicit alternatives have performed well with supplied addressing,
operations or authority. Those are legitimate capabilities to retain and
account for, not handicaps to remove from a control. The remaining opportunity
is to learn useful behavior where there is meaningful work after evidence becomes
available. Public research already demonstrates trained reading and reasoning
on counterfactual contexts. Another generic possibility demonstration would add
little. The question is durable complete usefulness and cost through corrections
from an inherited experience history, compared with developed contextual lessons.

One possible setting is repeated application of changing service instructions:
experience teaches how to combine a case with an exception, while current rules
and facts remain explicit. This is an illustration, not a required workload.
Choose public artifacts, natural version histories or a controlled setting for
what their outcomes would explain. Workload discovery belongs to this study.

## Starting expectations

Preserve these prospective root expectations and assess them alongside any
locally developed predictions. They are untested; no positive effect is required.

- **EU1 — transfer of use:** Learning from varied evidence-dependent cases can
  improve complete performance on fresh cases whose answers cannot be recovered
  from the old answers alone. The root expects some transfer of evidence-use
  behavior; familiar-answer recall or formatting improvement is insufficient.
- **EU2 — correction durability:** Experience that requires consulting current
  evidence should retain more of its benefit through corrections than experience
  that permits answer memorization. This is a relative prediction, not perfect
  compliance or superiority to contextual lessons. Benefit disappearing at the
  first correction would sharply limit useful lifetime.
- **EU3 — conditional repayment:** Acquisition advantage is likely to need
  repeated reuse to repay; competent contextual lessons should remain competitive
  at low reuse. Without a complete-quality advantage or lower recurring cost,
  repayment is not demonstrated. Hypothetical reuse cannot rescue a method that
  costs more on every use at comparable quality.

## Bounded contribution

Develop the smallest informative comparison and carry it through to an
explanation, a demonstrated limitation or a concrete resource constraint.
Choose methods, model, workload and finite resource limits locally. Begin with
closest methods and available artifacts; published evidence may resolve part
of the question without new runs. No particular algorithm, factorial grid or
large training campaign is mandated.

For an empirical claim, establish functioning acquisition and separate fresh
entities/bindings from corrections that change answers for familiar entities.
Assess revised and unchanged obligations, including complete outcomes rather
than only token loss, citation overlap or aggregate scores. Authority is explicit
and shared: the task is following authorized corrections, not believing arbitrary
retrieved text. Frozen reuse before further learning can identify durability;
subsequent repair, if needed, reveals its cost.

Include serious contextual lessons from the same available experience and
competent evidence access. Cheap executable solutions remain eligible. Disclose
supplied schemas, keys, operations and correction authority. Distinguish learned
use from memorized old answers, extra teacher information, longer generation or
better retrieval. If teacher traces are acquired, allow equivalent information
to the contextual alternative. These are inferential needs, not an instruction
to run every conceivable arm.

Account for acquisition, lesson construction, evidence updates, reading,
verification, repair and inference in their native units. Separate deployment
cost from experimental search. A strong explicit solution or a bounded acquisition
explanation can be a successful contribution. Preserve failed attempts; do not
stop at finite gradients or one unsuccessful minimally developed recipe. Test
claims developed during diagnosis on fresh material.
